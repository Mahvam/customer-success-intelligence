# National Mining Corp.

## Customer Overview

Our most strategic customer — a large mining company on a 3-year multi-year agreement. They're using the platform to improve screening for remote mining roles where safety, reliability, culture, and long-term fit aren't just preferences — they're life-or-death requirements.

## Customer Type

Enterprise / Complex — multi-year contract, high-touch onboarding, 20+ positions to configure, multiple stakeholders involved.

## Why This Account Matters

A worker at this company died on their first day at a remote job site. They lacked the situational awareness the environment demanded. That incident is the backdrop for everything we do with this account. When they say conscientiousness matters, they mean it in a way that most companies don't. Every assessment we build for them has to reflect the real stakes of putting someone in a remote, safety-critical role.

## What Happened

We started onboarding with the goal of building blueprints for 20 positions. The HR generalist, who was our main point of contact, was responsible for working with each hiring manager to gather the specific tasks, traits, and interview questions for every role. She got overwhelmed. With 20 positions and multiple managers to coordinate with, she reached out and told us it was going to take 2-3 weeks before she could get us what we needed.

Meanwhile, we had met 2-3 times early on and they had planned to start inviting candidates to take the assessments — but then they just got really busy, and momentum stalled.

## Key Risks

- Complexity of onboarding across 20+ roles and multiple stakeholders
- The HR generalist is a single point of contact carrying the weight of coordination across managers
- If momentum dies between meetings, the whole implementation timeline stretches
- High expectations due to the multi-year commitment and the safety stakes involved
- If initial setup drags, confidence in the platform erodes before they've seen results

## Current Strategy

Stop trying to boil the ocean. Focus on milestones, not volume. Rather than setting up 20 positions at once, tackle one at a time. Each working session should move one tangible thing forward: build one role, finalize one set of questions, or review one set of results.

Set up a cadence of weekly working sessions for 6-8 weeks upfront — not status calls, but actual working time together. This keeps momentum alive and prevents the account from going dark between touchpoints.

## Next Steps

- Establish weekly working sessions on the calendar for the next 6-8 weeks
- Start with one role, get it fully configured and live
- Support the HR generalist by providing the stakeholder input template so she can gather manager input more efficiently
- Align on interview questions and scoring approach for the first role before moving to the next
- Review early candidate results together to demonstrate value quickly

## What I Learned

Two big lessons from this account. First: with larger, complex customers, trying to set up everything at once overwhelms them. The person doing the coordination work internally has their own job — they can't dedicate weeks to gathering input from 20 managers. The milestone approach (one position at a time, progress over perfection) came directly from watching this account struggle under the weight of the full rollout.

Second: you have to set the meeting cadence upfront. We met a few times early on, then there was a gap, and momentum died. If I'd put 6-8 weekly sessions on the calendar from day one, we wouldn't have lost those weeks. That's now standard in the enterprise playbook.

## Expansion Opportunity

Once initial roles are live and producing results, there's a clear path to expanding across additional mining and maintenance positions. The long-term opportunity is standardizing their hiring practices across the company and measurably improving workforce quality and safety outcomes — which is exactly what they signed a 3-year deal to achieve.
