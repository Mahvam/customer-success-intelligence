# Top Customer Insights

These are patterns I've observed working directly with every customer account. They're not hypothetical — each one came from a specific situation that taught me something.

## The Talent Shortage Is the Elephant in the Room

The biggest pattern across all customers right now: they're stalling because qualified candidates aren't coming through the door. Our assessment is designed to tell companies who's qualified and who's not — but if the pipeline is empty, the platform doesn't get used. Trades companies are so desperate to fill seats that some have told us they just need "somebody with a pulse."

This is something the entire team has only recently acknowledged as a systemic issue, and it's a real strategic concern. We're still working through what this means for our positioning and our CS strategy.

## Pilot Customers Aren't Sold — They're Evaluating

If a customer was 100% sure about the product, they wouldn't sign a pilot. They'd commit to a multi-year deal. Pilots are evaluations with a ticking clock, and they require *more* proactive engagement, not less. Both of our pilot accounts went through the same cycle: onboarded on a position, went silent, turned out the position was on hold. If you're not reaching out and ready to pivot, the pilot expires without conversion.

## Every Company Names Their Positions Differently

During a live onboarding session, I was building a job blueprint when the tasks didn't line up with the position title the customer had given us. What they called an "industrial engineer" was actually an industrial mechanic. We have a library of 60,000+ blueprints, but even with that many, customers can't always find what they need because the naming conventions don't match. The fix: always lead with tasks and traits, not titles. Ask "what does this person actually do every day?" before selecting a template.

## Complex Customers Get Overwhelmed Fast

Our enterprise customer with 20+ positions to configure stalled because the HR generalist couldn't keep up with gathering input from every hiring manager. The lesson: don't try to set up everything at once. Focus on milestones — one position at a time — and provide tools (like the stakeholder input template) that make the coordination work easier.

## Meeting Cadence Has to Be Locked In Early

When there's a gap between onboarding meetings, accounts go dark. People get busy, other priorities take over, and momentum dies. Setting up 6-8 weekly working sessions on the calendar from day one prevents this. I learned this after watching an enterprise account slip for weeks between touchpoints.

## Positioning as Replacement, Not Addition

Customers adopt when the platform replaces an existing step in their process — specifically the initial phone screen. When they perceive it as an extra step on top of what they're already doing, they push back. Our most active customer uses the assessment *before* interviews begin, and that's why it stuck. This positioning is now central to every onboarding conversation.

## The Dashboard Tells a Story

Stepping back and looking at the big picture matters as much as the details. When I look at a customer's dashboard and see candidates going from assessment directly to hire with no interview stage marked, that tells me something is off — either they're skipping a step or not logging it in the platform. Either way, it's a conversation worth having because it affects the analytics we need to demonstrate ROI.

## What Drives Real Engagement

- The customer has an immediate, active hiring need
- The platform is positioned as part of their existing workflow, not a separate tool
- Clear guidance on when and how to send the assessment
- Early demonstration of value — either through real candidates or sample evaluations
- Consistent usage on at least one role before expanding

## Early Expansion Signals

- Customer consistently uses the platform on at least one role
- Customer references time saved or improved screening quality
- Customer asks follow-up questions about results or scoring
- Additional team members get involved in reviewing assessments
- Customer asks about applying the platform to more roles before you suggest it
