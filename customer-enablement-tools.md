# Customer Enablement Tools

## The Problem These Solve

The most common reason customers stall during onboarding is not disinterest. It is that gathering role specific input from hiring managers is a coordination bottleneck. The internal champion is supposed to work with each manager to define the tasks, traits, and interview questions for their roles. But managers are busy, they do not always know how to articulate what they need, and the request can feel overwhelming.

I saw this play out directly with a large enterprise account. The internal coordinator was responsible for gathering input across more than twenty positions and multiple managers. She hit a wall and told us she would need two to three weeks. That is when I built these tools, to reduce the perceived effort and keep things moving.

## Stakeholder Input Template

The goal: unlock the bottleneck by making it dead simple for a manager to give you what you need.

> Hi NAME,
>
> We are working on improving how we screen candidates for your team. Could you provide quick input on the below?
>
> 1. Key Tasks (Top 3 to 5): What are the most important things this person does daily?
> 2. What makes someone successful in this role? (Example: reliable, safety focused, adaptable, and so on)
> 3. Interview Question (Scenario Based): Example, "Tell me about a time when..."
> 4. One or Two Follow Up Questions: How did you handle it? What would you do differently?
>
> This does not need to be perfect, quick input is great.

The key design choice: "this does not need to be perfect." Managers freeze when they think you are asking them to write a formal job description. Lowering the bar gets you 80% of what you need, and you can refine the rest during the working session.

## Traits and Scenario Template

A structured framework for gathering the specific tasks and scenario based questions needed to configure role assessments. It can be filled out collaboratively during a working session or sent asynchronously using the outreach template above.

The most important thing I have learned about this process: lead with tasks, not job titles. Every company names its positions differently. I once had a customer call a role "industrial engineer" when the actual daily work was closer to industrial mechanic. If you start by asking "what does this person actually do?" instead of "what is the job title?", you get to the right blueprint faster.

## Why These Tools Matter

Without them, accounts stall in the configuration phase. The internal champion burns out trying to coordinate, meetings get delayed, and by the time everything is ready, the customer's momentum is gone. These tools exist to keep the implementation moving forward, one position at a time.
