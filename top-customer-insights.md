# Top Customer Insights

These are patterns I observed working directly across customer accounts. None of them are hypothetical. Each came from a specific situation that taught me something, and each one now shapes how I engage.

## The Talent Shortage Shapes Adoption

The biggest pattern across accounts: customers stall when qualified candidates are not coming through the pipeline. A screening assessment tells a company who is qualified and who is not, but if the pipeline is empty, the tool does not get used. In some sectors the shortage is severe enough that employers feel pressed to fill seats almost regardless of fit. That reality matters, because even a strong screening product cannot solve a supply problem. Recognizing it changed how I set expectations with customers and where I focus my effort, and I now flag it early rather than discover it late.

## Pilot Customers Are Evaluating, Not Sold

If a customer were fully sure about the product, they would not run a pilot, they would sign a longer agreement. Pilots are evaluations with a ticking clock, and they need more proactive engagement, not less. A repeated pattern across pilot accounts: onboarded on a role, activity went quiet, and the role turned out to be on hold. If you are not reaching out and ready to pivot to another open role, the pilot expires without converting.

## Every Company Names Its Positions Differently

During a live onboarding session I was building a job blueprint when the tasks did not line up with the position title the customer had given us. What they called an "industrial engineer" was closer to an industrial mechanic. Even with a large library of blueprints to choose from, customers cannot always find the right one, because naming conventions do not match across companies. The fix: lead with tasks and traits, not titles. Ask what this person actually does every day before selecting a template.

## Complex Accounts Get Overwhelmed Fast

An enterprise account with more than twenty positions to configure stalled because the internal coordinator could not keep up with gathering input from every hiring manager. The lesson: do not try to set everything up at once. Focus on milestones, one position at a time, and provide tools like a stakeholder input template that make the coordination work easier.

## Meeting Cadence Has to Be Locked In Early

When there is a gap between onboarding meetings, accounts go dark. People get busy, other priorities take over, and momentum dies. Booking six to eight weekly working sessions on the calendar from day one prevents this. I learned it after watching an account slip for weeks between touchpoints.

## Position as a Replacement, Not an Addition

Customers adopt when the platform replaces an existing step in their process, specifically the initial phone screen. When they see it as an extra step on top of what they already do, they push back. The customers who embed the assessment before interviews begin are the ones where it sticks. This positioning is now central to every onboarding conversation.

## The Dashboard Tells a Story

Stepping back to look at the big picture matters as much as the details. When I look at a customer's dashboard and see candidates going from assessment straight to hire with no interview stage marked, that tells me something is off. Either they are skipping a step or not logging it in the platform. Either way it is a conversation worth having, because it affects the analytics we use to demonstrate value.

## What Drives Real Engagement

* The customer has an immediate, active hiring need
* The platform is positioned as part of their existing workflow, not a separate tool
* Clear guidance on when and how to send the assessment
* Early demonstration of value, through real candidates or sample evaluations
* Consistent usage on at least one role before expanding

## Early Expansion Signals

* The customer consistently uses the platform on at least one role
* The customer references time saved or improved screening quality
* The customer asks follow up questions about results or scoring
* Additional team members get involved in reviewing assessments
* The customer asks about applying the platform to more roles before you suggest it
