# Customer Enablement Tools

## The Problem These Solve

The most common reason customers stall during onboarding isn't disinterest — it's that gathering role-specific input from hiring managers is a coordination bottleneck. The HR person or internal champion is supposed to work with each manager to define the tasks, traits, and interview questions for their roles. But managers are busy, they don't always know how to articulate what they need, and the request can feel overwhelming.

I saw this play out directly with our enterprise mining customer. The HR generalist was responsible for coordinating input across 20+ positions and multiple managers. She hit a wall and told us she'd need 2-3 weeks. That's when I built these tools — to reduce the perceived effort and keep things moving.

## Stakeholder Input Template

The goal: unlock the bottleneck by making it dead simple for a manager to give you what you need.

> Hi **NAME**,
>
> We're working on improving how we screen candidates for your team. Could you provide quick input on the below?
>
> 1. **Key Tasks (Top 3–5):** What are the most important things this person does daily?
> 2. **What makes someone successful in this role?** (Example: reliable, safety-focused, adaptable, etc.)
> 3. **Interview Question (Scenario-Based):** Example: "Tell me about a time when..."
> 4. **1–2 Follow-Up Questions:** How did you handle it? What would you do differently?
>
> This doesn't need to be perfect, quick input is great.

The key design choice: "this doesn't need to be perfect." Managers freeze when they think you're asking them to write a formal job description. Lowering the bar gets you 80% of what you need, and you can refine from there during the working session.

## Traits/Scenario Template

A structured framework for gathering the specific tasks and scenario-based questions needed to configure role assessments. This can be filled out collaboratively during a working session or sent asynchronously using the outreach template above.

The most important thing I've learned about this process: lead with tasks, not job titles. Every company names their positions differently. I had a customer call a role "industrial engineer" when the actual day-to-day work was industrial mechanic. If you start by asking "what does this person actually do?" instead of "what's the job title?", you get to the right blueprint faster.

## Why These Tools Matter

Without them, accounts stall in the configuration phase. The internal champion burns out trying to coordinate, meetings get delayed, and by the time everything is ready, the customer's momentum is gone. These tools exist to keep the implementation moving forward, one position at a time.
