# Midwest Manufacturing Co.

## Customer Overview

Pilot customer in the industrial/manufacturing space focused on maintenance hiring. Signed on to evaluate the platform with a single position.

## Customer Type

Pilot — paying a small fee to evaluate before committing to a multi-year agreement. If they convert, the pilot fee is credited toward their contract.

## What Happened

We onboarded them with a specific position — what they called an industrial engineer role. During the live blueprint-building session, I noticed the tasks they were describing didn't align with the tasks our platform was surfacing for that job title. It clicked mid-session: this company was calling the position something different than what the role actually was. Once they described the day-to-day work in more detail, it was clear this was an industrial mechanic, not an industrial engineer. We pivoted on the spot, selected a different job blueprint, and the tasks lined up.

After onboarding, there was no activity on the account for over a week — no candidates invited, no assessments taken. I reached out to check in, and they told me the position had been put on hold due to an employee on medical leave. Rather than let the pilot clock run while nothing was happening, I suggested we pivot and build an assessment for a position they actually had open.

## Key Risks

- No active hiring means no platform usage, and the pilot timer is running
- If we wait for them, momentum dies and we lose the account
- The customer hasn't experienced enough value yet to justify converting

## Current Strategy

Don't wait. Create value even when hiring is paused. Run sample candidates through the platform so the customer can see how the assessment works and what strong vs. weak responses look like — before they have real candidates.

## Next Steps

- Run 2–3 sample candidates through the platform
- Walk through the results together so they see the value firsthand
- Pivot to any open position as soon as one becomes available

## What I Learned

Two things from this account. First: every company names their positions differently, so aligning on tasks and traits matters more than matching a job title. I caught this in real time during onboarding and it changed how I approach every blueprint session now — I lead with "tell me what this person actually does" before selecting a template. Second: pilot customers go silent when their initial position falls through, and if you're not proactively reaching out, you'll lose them.

## Expansion Opportunity

If the customer sees value in candidate comparison and insights through the sample evaluation, there's opportunity to expand across maintenance roles and standardize their screening process on the platform.
