# Customer Intelligence Overview

## Why This Exists

When I joined as the founding CS hire at an early stage hiring assessment startup, there was no system for tracking customer health, no segmented engagement strategy, and no shared knowledge about what was working and what was not. The information existed, in conversations, in calls, in my head, but it was not documented, structured, or usable by anyone else.

I built this system because I knew from earlier startup experience that understanding customers strategically is what separates companies that retain and grow accounts from companies that lose them. I raised it with our CTO in a 1:1, started building, and when I showed him the finished product, his response was that it went well beyond what he expected.

## How It Works

The system has three connected layers:

**Customer Profiles** capture what is happening at the individual account level: the real situation, the risks, the active strategy, and what I have learned from each account. These are living documents that evolve as the account evolves.

**Customer Playbooks** define how we engage specific customer types based on observed patterns. The segments are Pilot (evaluating, time limited, high risk of stalling), Enterprise (multi year, complex, high touch), and Complex Multi Year (milestone driven, customization heavy). Each playbook was built from real account experience, not templates.

**Cross Customer Insights** aggregate what I am seeing across accounts into organizational knowledge: recurring pain points, adoption blockers, engagement drivers, and expansion signals. This is where individual account lessons become team wide strategy.

## What We Are Seeing

The patterns that have emerged across accounts:

* Pilot customers stall when their initial role goes on hold, and if you are not proactively reaching out, you lose them
* A broader talent shortage in the market affects platform usage regardless of how good the product is
* Enterprise customers get overwhelmed when you try to configure too many positions at once
* Customers adopt when the platform replaces their phone screen step, not when it is positioned as an add on
* Early demonstration of value, through real or sample candidates, is the strongest predictor of engagement
* Companies name positions differently, so aligning on tasks and traits matters more than matching a job title

## What We Are Doing About It

* Pivoting pilot customers to new roles immediately when the first one falls through, rather than waiting
* Running sample candidate evaluations to create value even when active hiring is delayed
* Using the milestone approach with complex accounts, one position at a time, weekly working sessions
* Positioning the platform as a replacement for phone screens in every onboarding conversation
* Providing stakeholder input templates to reduce the coordination burden on internal champions
* Standardizing these approaches through documented playbooks so the strategy is consistent and scalable

## Why This Matters

This system moves the team from reactive account management, responding to problems after they happen, to a proactive, pattern driven model where we anticipate risk, standardize our best responses, and capture what we learn so it benefits every account going forward. With a small customer base where every account matters, that structure is the difference between growth and churn.
